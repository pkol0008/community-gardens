AUSTRALIA · COMMUNITY · GARDENING

Growing Together

Exploring Australia's community gardens: where they are, what they look like, and how they are recorded as being accessed.

This exploration uses a community garden directory. The charts describe the records available in that dataset, which may not represent every community garden in Australia today.

01 / WHERE

Where are the gardens?

Explore the geographic distribution of listed gardens. The state shading shows the number of directory records, while the circles show individual gardens and their recorded sizes.

02 / ACCESS

Are the gardens open to the public?

Explore the recorded public-access categories and where those gardens are located. These categories reflect the directory entries rather than independently verified opening policies.

03 / GARDEN TYPES

What kinds of gardens are listed?

Compare the frequency of the garden types recorded in the directory. More records do not necessarily mean a type is more common across the entire country.

04 / HISTORY

When were the gardens established?

See how reported establishment years are distributed across the listed gardens. This chart counts gardens by their reported establishment year; it does not estimate the total number of gardens that existed in each historical year.

05 / SIZE

How large are the gardens?

A histogram groups recorded garden areas into ranges, revealing the spread of garden sizes and whether the directory includes particularly large sites.

06 / ACCESS & TYPE

Does recorded access vary by garden type?

Compare the proportions of public-access categories within each garden type. Some categories or types may have relatively few records, so interpret small groups cautiously.

07 / REGIONAL PATTERNS

Which garden types appear across the states?

The heatmap compares counts across states and territories. It helps reveal where directory records are concentrated, but does not adjust for population or the completeness of the directory in each region.

08 / REGIONAL SIZE

How does garden size vary by state?

Box plots compare the distribution of recorded garden areas. The boxes show the middle half of the observations, while whiskers and outliers help reveal variation within each region.
