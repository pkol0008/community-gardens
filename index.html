Australia · Community · Gardening

Growing Together

A visual exploration of Australia's community gardens: where they are, how large they are, when they were established, and how they are recorded as being accessed.

This project uses a community garden directory as its primary dataset. The records provide a useful view of community gardening across Australia, but they may not represent every garden operating today.

01 / Where

Where are the gardens?

We begin with the geographic distribution of community garden records. State shading shows how records are distributed between regions, while individual circles identify garden locations and their recorded sizes.

02 / Access

Are the gardens open to the public?

Location alone does not tell us how these spaces can be used. The directory records several forms of public access, ranging from gardens open to the general public to spaces reserved for members or clients.

These categories come from the directory and should not be interpreted as independently verified opening policies.

03 / Garden settings

What kinds of garden spaces are represented?

Most records describe general community gardens, while the directory also contains school gardens, city farms, verge gardens and a small number of other shared growing spaces.

These categories describe the kinds of spaces represented in the directory rather than the national prevalence of each setting.

04 / History

When were these gardens established?

The gardens represented in the directory span many decades. Their reported establishment years show when individual sites first appeared and help reveal periods when more of the recorded gardens were established.

This chart counts records by establishment year. It does not represent the total number of gardens operating in Australia during each year.

05 / Size

How large are the gardens?

Community gardens can occupy very different amounts of space. Grouping recorded areas into ranges reveals the overall size distribution and shows how common smaller and larger sites are within the dataset.

06 / Regional size

Which states have larger gardens?

Typical garden size also differs between regions. Using the median reduces the influence of extremely large sites and makes the central garden size in each state easier to compare.

07 / Size distributions

How do garden sizes differ between states?

A median provides a useful summary, but it can hide variation within each region. These distributions show the broader spread of recorded garden sizes across states and territories.

The horizontal scale is logarithmic so that both smaller gardens and unusually large sites remain visible without extreme values compressing the rest of the distribution.

08 / Access & setting

Does public access vary by garden setting?

Garden setting and public access can also be considered together. This view compares the proportion of access categories within each kind of garden space represented in the directory.

Some settings contain relatively few records, so proportions for these smaller groups should be interpreted cautiously.

09 / Regional patterns

How do garden settings vary across states?

The mix of garden settings is not identical across Australia. This heatmap compares the number of records for each setting across states and territories, making regional concentrations easier to see.

These values are raw directory counts and do not account for differences in population or data coverage between regions.

10 / Population context

Where are garden records most concentrated relative to population?

Raw garden counts can favour more populous states simply because more people live there. To provide another perspective, the directory is compared with state and territory population figures from the 2021 Australian Census.

This map shows the number of garden directory records per 100,000 residents. It represents the concentration of records in the dataset, not the true number of operating community gardens per resident.

Conclusion

A varied community garden landscape

Taken together, the records show considerable variation in the location, size, age and recorded accessibility of community garden spaces across Australia.

Looking beyond simple totals also changes the picture. Garden sizes vary both within and between states, while population-adjusted comparisons provide a different geographic perspective from raw counts alone.

These findings should therefore be interpreted as patterns within the available directory rather than a complete census of every community garden currently operating in Australia.
